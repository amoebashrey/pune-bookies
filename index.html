what this is

A reading community, but not really.

A few hundred of us turn up on a Sunday morning, find a tree, and read for two hours. Some of us read poetry. Some read graphic novels. Most of us are quiet. A few of us stay back to talk after.

We started in early 2024 because reading had become hard to protect from everything else. The phone. The laundry. The rest of life. It turned out a lot of people in Pune were having the same problem. We're closer to 250 on a typical Sunday now.

Sunday is the cathedral. We don't charge for the cathedral.

We don't run ads in our WhatsApp groups. We don't put the page-guessing game on an app. We're stubborn about the small things, which we think is the only way to be useful about the larger ones.

We're one chapter of a network — Bombay, Bengaluru, Jaipur, and a few more on the way. Each city runs its own Sunday, its own way. We share a name and a way of doing things, and not much else.

how Sunday actually works

It's simpler than you think.

Sat, 4:05 PM

The location goes out

Every Saturday at 4:05 in the afternoon, the next morning's spot is shared in the WhatsApp groups. Could be a park, could be a farm, could be a botanical garden. It changes weekly.

7:45 AM

You're heading there

Bring a book. A water bottle. Sunglasses if you need them. Something to sit on if grass and you aren't friends. That's it.

8:00 AM

Show up. Hello.

There's a small welcome table. A bookmark for you. A basket for the page-guessing game — drop in your guess for how many pages we'll collectively read today. We mean it. Pen and paper.

8:30 AM

Pick a spot. Read.

This is not a book club. We don't discuss the book. Everyone reads their own thing. The silence is the point. Two hours of it, more or less, with the wind doing whatever it does.

9:00 AM

Page guesses get collected

Someone walks past the basket and takes the slips. We add up the pages everyone read. The closest guess wins something small — a bookmark from a previous week, a postcard. We don't make a thing of it.

9:45 AM

A gentle wrap-up

Someone walks through the readers letting people know we'll wrap soon. Group photo at 9:50 if you want to be in it. If you don't, you step to the side. No explanation needed.

10:00 AM

Some people leave. Some stay.

The official Sunday is over. Some head off to breakfast. Some stand around and talk about what they read, what they didn't, the weather, whatever. A few people meet a friend they didn't know they had.

small notes

Three Sundays.

The first Sunday I came alone.

I almost didn't go. I'd told a friend I would and she bailed and I thought, well that's that, I'll go next week. I went anyway. I was the third person there. By 8:30 there were eighty of us. I read forty pages of a book I'd been carrying around for three months. I didn't talk to anyone. I came back the next Sunday.

— Sneha, regular since April

The morning the geese came.

We were at a venue we hadn't used before. Around 9:15, three geese walked across the lawn, in a perfect line, past about a hundred and twenty of us reading. Nobody made a sound. The geese kept walking. Somebody took a photo. We talk about that morning more than we talk about most Sundays. Nothing happened. That was the whole event.

— Aman, regular

What I keep finding here.

The first month I started this I thought it was about the books. By the third month I was certain it was about the people. By the sixth I'd stopped trying to figure out what it was about and started just showing up. I think that's the actual thing — a place to show up that doesn't ask you for anything in exchange.

— Tanvi, on starting this
things people quietly wonder

Probably your question.

When and where does it happen?

Sundays, 8–10 AM. The location moves every week. We share the spot on Saturday at 4:05 PM, via WhatsApp.

Is it free?

Yes. Always.

Do I need to RSVP?

No. Show up. Joining the WhatsApp just gets you the Saturday location ping.

What do I bring?

A book. That’s the only requirement. Some people bring water, coffee, a mat. We don’t.

Can I come alone? Will I have to talk to anyone?

Yes, you can come alone. Most people do. We read in silence for two hours. No one has to introduce themselves, share a passage, or perform anything.

What if it rains?

Then we don’t. The WhatsApp tells you by Saturday night.

How do I get on the WhatsApp?

DM us on Instagram, @pune_bookies. One of us will add you.

Are there other cities?

Bombay, Bengaluru, Jaipur.
